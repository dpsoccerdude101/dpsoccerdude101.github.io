Why not A

A is not worse. It is the higher-ceiling, higher-risk choice, and it has messier third-shot roles.

A's pairs put away more shots (11.1 and 12.3 per 100) and miss more (18.6 and 18.8 per 100). B's put away less (10.7 and 9.1) and miss less (13.6 and 12.7 per 100).

  • Peter's third-shot drive with Brooke misses 55.6% (10 of 18).
  • Lindsay's drop with Dennis misses 40.0% (6 of 15).
  • Dennis's dink with Lindsay misses 38.5% (5 of 13).

No A pair ever played a B pair, so there is no head-to-head. Play A when you need to chase a power mismatch or the opponents hand out free balls.

What would change our mind

These are the triggers, in the order you would meet them. If one is true, play what it names.

  1. No information on the opponents and you need both mixed wins. Play B.Lower error, more kitchen arrivals, clearer roles.
  2. You need to aim power at one specific opposing pair, and that pair is weak or error-prone. Play A.A lets you put D+L’s power on the weak pair.
  3. The opposing pairs are strong and steady and give away few free points. Play B.B wins by attrition, while A’s misses get punished.
  4. The opposing pairs are weak and error-prone and hand out free balls. Play A.A’s finishing converts free balls that B may let drop.
  5. The format rewards margin or longer games. Play A.More putaways and more finishing power.
  6. The games are short, win by 2, and weighted equally. Play B.Lower risk and a higher floor.
  7. Dennis fixes his dink, or Lindsay fixes her drop with Dennis. Play A.Removes A’s main weakness and raises its ceiling.
  8. Brooke fixes her drop, or Lindsay fixes her drive. Play B.Raises B’s floor and ceiling further.

The favorite/underdog math is worth almost nothing here. Do not use it as a reason.

What could be fooling us

  • The shot labels are imperfect. In 60 of 219 rallies (27.4%) the final-shot label disagrees with who won. Everything here uses the rally winner to correct the final shot, so these fault rates differ slightly from the raw tags. The pattern holds after the correction.
  • The opponents were not the same. A pairs faced opponents who put away 11.7 shots per 100; B pairs faced 9.9. Some of B's lower error rate may simply be less pressure. Read the fault advantage as suggestive, not proven.
  • Six games is a small sample. Three per pairing. A few more games could flip the outcome lean.
  • No A pair played a B pair. There is no matchup data. This is a tradeoff, not a proven ranking.

What we assumed

  • Two mixed games per team match, equal weight. Each pair faces one opposing mixed pair.
  • Side-out scoring to 11, win by 2. G3 was to 15; G7 was shortened (8–7). Margins were normalized where used, so the raw scores do not compare across games.
  • No order or fatigue effects, and the opposing pairs are comparable in strength.
  • The goal is the most mixed-game wins over time, not the most points.
  • If the format switches to rally scoring, point-differential weighting, or a dream breaker, the risk/ceiling tradeoff changes and the switch table needs a rerun.
